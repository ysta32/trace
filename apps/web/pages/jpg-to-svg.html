Understand what changes when you trace a JPEG

A JPG stores pixels using lossy compression. SVG can describe the boundaries of colored shapes instead. Converting between them means interpreting the image, not simply changing its container. Trace creates a vector representation that you can scale and edit, but it cannot recover every detail that JPEG compression has removed.

This distinction matters most for small logos and screenshots. Compression can leave block patterns or halos around sharp edges. A tracer may interpret those artifacts as real features. If you can obtain the original PNG or vector file, use it. If JPEG is your only source, start from the highest-quality copy and avoid saving it repeatedly before conversion.

How to turn JPG into SVG

  1. Choose a clear source. Use the largest useful original rather than a thumbnail. Prepare any crop in an image editor so the subject, rather than unnecessary background, receives your attention.
  2. Open it in Trace. Processing runs locally through WebAssembly. Your image is never uploaded for conversion, making it practical to work with unpublished artwork on your own device.
  3. Pick a preset. Use logo for flat graphics, line art for drawings, pixel art for intentionally blocky artwork, or photo poster for a stylized photographic result.
  4. Adjust the colors. Start with automatic palette and color count detection, then compare a smaller palette with a more detailed one. Preserve meaningful regions rather than every compression shade.
  5. Review and export. Use the split-slider preview, edit the palette, and select shapes to recolor or delete. Export SVG when the interpretation matches your intended use.

Reduce noise without erasing the subject

Inspect high-contrast boundaries first: dark lettering against a pale background, thin outlines, and small highlights are useful checkpoints. If you see many tiny pieces around an otherwise simple edge, try fewer colors. If nearby objects merge together, restore enough colors to separate them. A good trace balances readability and fidelity instead of maximizing the number of shapes.

Photographs contain continuous tonal changes, texture, and lighting. Translating all of that into solid shapes can create a complicated file without preserving a natural photographic appearance. The photo poster preset is a starting point for an intentional graphic treatment. For an unchanged family photo or product photograph, the original raster format is often the more suitable choice.

JPG does not carry a transparent background. A white background in the source is made of actual pixels and may become part of the traced artwork. Inspect the result before removing shapes, especially when the subject itself contains white areas. Check the exported design against its planned background in the application where you will use it.

Finish in the format your workflow needs

Trace offers SVG, PDF, EPS, DXF, and PNG export. SVG is convenient for scalable illustrations and editing paths; PNG is useful when you need a rendered bitmap. Open your exported file in the destination software before delivery, since format support and the handling of shapes can vary between applications.

Batch conversion can save repetitive setup when you have several similar images. Review a representative result before processing a large group, and check exceptions afterward. For automation, Trace includes a CLI and an npm package installed with npm i trace-vectorizer. Its MIT-licensed source is available for inspection and adaptation.

Frequently asked questions

Are JPG and JPEG different here?

They are common filename extensions for the same image format. The tracing considerations are the same: source quality, compression artifacts, and the amount of visual detail.

Will conversion make a photo look identical?

Not necessarily. Vector tracing approximates visible regions with shapes. It is useful for stylized photographs, but fine textures and smooth shading may change.

Can I edit colors after tracing?

Yes. Trace supports palette editing and selecting individual shapes to recolor or delete. Inspect the preview before exporting so small details remain intentional.
